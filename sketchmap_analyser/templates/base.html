Sketch Map Analyser

What people draw from memory, measured.

Sketch maps can be considered externalizations of cognitive mental maps. SketchMapia compares them against a map of the real environment, so that studying them no longer means scoring every drawing by hand.

About

Researchers have long sought to systematically study how people perceive and understand space. Analysing sketch maps, though, has stayed a slow and largely manual process. SketchMapia is a method for doing it systematically, for use in the social and natural sciences.

The software helps an experimenter to analyse:

I

Which features the participant remembered

Of everything in the real environment, what appears in the drawing at all.

II

At which level of generalisation they recalled objects

How far they abstracted: a row of shops drawn as one block, a junction simplified, a whole area reduced to a single labelled shape.

III

Whether they recalled the correct spatial configuration

Given what they drew, whether it was arranged correctly.

Two drawings of one neighbourhood

These sketch maps of Gievenbeck in Münster were drawn from memory by two participants. Both kept Hensenstraße running east to west, both placed Rüschhausweg and Enschedeweg either side of the block between them, and both remembered the Wartburg Grundschule and St. Michael Kirche. Beyond that they diverge: one recalls shops, dormitories and side streets the other leaves out entirely.

Simplification is not error

A participant who draws four adjacent buildings as one block has not forgotten three of them. They have abstracted, and that is worth measuring in its own right rather than counting as a mistake.

So before any accuracy measure runs, SketchMapia applies the participant's own simplifications to the reference map. If they merged four buildings, those four are merged on the reference side too, and the comparison is made like for like. The kinds of simplification people make are themselves recorded, which is how generalisation becomes something you can study rather than something you have to correct for.
